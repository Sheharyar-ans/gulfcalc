1. The Reinstatement of Kuwait Family Visas under Decision No. 56/2024

In late January 2024, the Ministry of Interior promulgated Ministerial Decision No. 56 of 2024, ending a protracted multi-year moratorium on the issuance of family visit and dependent residency visas (Article 22 of the Aliens Residence Law). Promulgated under the directives of the Council of Ministers, the modern regulatory framework re-established expatriate family sponsorship under significantly tightened socio-economic thresholds designed to ensure demographic sustainability.

Prior to the suspension, expatriates could sponsor family members with a monthly salary threshold of 500 KWD. Decision No. 56 of 2024 raised this statutory minimum to 800 KWD per month, representing a 60% escalation. Crucially, the regulations mandate that this 800 KWD figure must be explicitly stated on the applicant's official work permit (Izn Amal) issued by the Public Authority for Manpower (PAM). Private contractual addendums or supplementary side agreements are not recognized during residency processing.

2. The Mandatory University Degree Qualification Mandate

In addition to the financial threshold, Ministerial Decision 56/2024 introduced a rigid educational requirement: the applicant must possess a recognized, authenticated university degree (Bachelor's level or higher). The academic specialization must demonstrate direct congruity with the profession recorded on their civil records and work permit.

To be accepted by the Residency Affairs Department, academic documents must undergo an exhaustive multi-stage legalization protocol:

  • Authentication by the Ministry of Higher Education and Ministry of Foreign Affairs in the originating nation.
  • Consular legalization by the Embassy of the State of Kuwait in the issuing country.
  • Final endorsement by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Consular Affairs Department) in Shuwaikh, Kuwait.

3. The 14 Exempted Professional Categories

Recognizing the critical importance of specialized human capital to national infrastructure, the Ministry of Interior exempted fourteen specialized professional categories from the 800 KWD minimum salary threshold (though profession verification remains enforced):

  1. Consultant physicians, surgeons, specialists, and licensed general medical doctors.
  2. Pharmacists and medical laboratory specialists.
  3. University professors, deans, and academic researchers in state-recognized tertiary institutions.
  4. School teachers and academic educators across public and licensed private schools.
  5. Judges, public prosecutors, and legal counsel registered with the Ministry of Justice.
  6. Engineers holding accredited engineering society memberships.
  7. Mosque imams, preachers, and licensed Islamic scholars.
  8. Civil aviation pilots, flight navigators, and marine captains.

4. Dhaman Health Insurance and Civil ID Fee Architecture

Once the entry visa is granted and the dependents arrive in Kuwait, statutory residency completion requires mandatory health insurance enrollment through the Health Assurance Hospitals Company (Dhaman) or Ministry of Health health centers. The statutory fee is fixed at 50 KWD per dependent per year.

Following health insurance payment, the sponsor must pay residency fees to the General Department of Residency: 10 KWD annually for the spouse and each child. Finally, registration with the Public Authority for Civil Information (PACI) requires 5 KWD for standard card printing (or 10 KWD for expedited electronic issuance) per dependent.

5. Age Limitations for Dependent Sponsorship

Under Article 22 residency rules, male children may remain under parental sponsorship up to the age of twenty-one (21) years, provided they are enrolled in a recognized university or higher institute in Kuwait or abroad. Upon reaching age 21, sons must either transfer to an employment visa (Article 18) or student visa.

Unmarried daughters can remain under paternal sponsorship on an Article 22 visa indefinitely, provided they remain unmarried and do not enter formal commercial employment.

Frequently Asked Questions: Kuwait Family Visas & Decision 56/2024

What is the minimum salary required to sponsor a family visa in Kuwait?
Under Ministry of Interior Ministerial Decision No. 56 of 2024, an expatriate must draw a minimum monthly wage of 800 KWD explicitly documented on their Public Authority for Manpower (PAM) work permit to sponsor a spouse and children on an Article 22 dependent visa.
Is a university degree mandatory for a Kuwait family visa?
Yes, Ministerial Decision No. 56/2024 requires the expatriate sponsor to possess an accredited university degree that directly matches their registered profession in Kuwait. The degree must be apostilled and attested by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
How much are annual health insurance (Dhaman) fees for dependents in Kuwait?
Annual health insurance through the Health Assurance Hospitals Company (Dhaman) or Ministry of Health is 50 KWD per dependent per year. This health insurance clearance is mandatory before the General Department of Residency will issue or renew an Article 22 residency stamp.
Are there exemptions to the 800 KWD Kuwait salary requirement?
Exemptions apply to 14 specific professional groups pursuant to ministerial directives, including medical doctors, university professors, judges, licensed engineers, and expatriates who were born in Kuwait and hold recognized specialized credentials.
Can an expat sponsor their parents on an Article 22 visa in Kuwait?
Sponsorship of parents is subject to exceptional Ministry of Interior scrutiny and higher financial thresholds, typically requiring a salary exceeding 1,000 KWD, commercial private medical insurance coverage, and formal humanitarian approval from the Residency Affairs Undersecretary.
