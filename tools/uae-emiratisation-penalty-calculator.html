1. Legislative Authority and the NAFIS Emiratisation Mandate

In May 2022, the UAE Cabinet issued Resolution No. (19/M) of 2022, introducing a mandatory, progressive Emiratisation quota framework across private sector enterprises operating in mainland UAE jurisdictions. Supervised by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE) alongside the Emirati Human Resources Competitiveness Council (NAFIS), this initiative aims to accelerate the sustainable integration of UAE national talent into high-value private sector professions.

The overarching legislative objective requires commercial establishments to achieve an aggregate 10% Emiratisation quota in skilled positions by the close of 2026. To ensure steady, orderly absorption without disrupting corporate operations, MoHRE instituted a bi-annual evaluation mechanism requiring enterprises to expand their Emirati workforce by 1% semi-annually (2% per calendar year).

2. Quota Schedules Across Enterprise Scales

The application of statutory Emiratisation targets is stratified according to the establishment's total skilled labor force:

  • Establishments with 50+ Skilled Employees: These enterprises must demonstrate a cumulative 2% net expansion in skilled UAE national employment every calendar year (1% evaluated on June 30 and 1% evaluated on December 31). Targets are calculated strictly against the establishment's registered skilled workforce (occupational levels 1 to 5).
  • Small and Medium Enterprises (20 to 49 Employees): Pursuant to Cabinet Decision No. 459/2023, targeted SMEs operating within fourteen high-growth economic activities (including information technology, real estate, financial services, healthcare, and engineering consultancies) are mandated to recruit at least one UAE citizen in 2024 and a second UAE citizen in 2025.

3. Escalating Financial Contribution (Penalty) Scale

Establishments that fail to achieve their prescribed semi-annual or annual hiring quotas do not face discretionary administrative fines; instead, they are subject to statutory financial contributions collected directly by MoHRE into the Human Resources Development Fund. The penalty structure escalates automatically by 1,000 AED per month each calendar year:

4. Definition of Skilled Positions under MoHRE Regulations

Emiratisation ratios are not evaluated against total headcount; they apply exclusively to workers categorized within Skill Levels 1 through 5 under the official International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO-08) adopted by MoHRE:

  1. Level 1: Legislators, senior corporate officials, managing directors, and chief executives.
  2. Level 2: Scientific, technical, legal, engineering, and medical professionals.
  3. Level 3: Technicians and associate professionals across commercial, banking, and creative disciplines.
  4. Level 4: Specialized customer service, administrative, clerical, and secretarial occupations.
  5. Level 5: Specialized service and sales personnel.

To count toward the establishment's statutory quota, the UAE citizen must be registered on the electronic NAFIS portal, possess an active work permit issued by MoHRE, receive a monthly salary of at least 4,000 AED, and be formally enrolled with the General Pension and Social Security Authority (GPSSA) or the Abu Dhabi Pension Fund.

5. Severe Penalties for Bogus Emiratisation (Tawteen Souri)

Cabinet Resolution No. 44 of 2023 established severe administrative and criminal sanctions to eradicate nominal or fraudulent Emiratisation (commonly known as Tawteen Souri). Fake Emiratisation occurs when an establishment registers a UAE citizen as an active employee without assigning real tasks, or to artificially manipulate compliance metrics.

Establishments caught engaging in bogus hiring face administrative fines ranging from 20,000 to 100,000 AED per fraudulent employee, mandatory restitution of all NAFIS wage subsidies, reclassification into MoHRE Tier 3 (the lowest compliance category, multiplying work permit fees), and referral of corporate executives to the Public Prosecution for financial fraud.

Frequently Asked Questions: UAE Emiratisation Penalties & MoHRE Rules

What is the penalty for failing to meet UAE Emiratisation quotas?
For establishments with 50 or more skilled staff, the MoHRE non-compliance penalty is 8,000 AED per month (96,000 AED annually) per unhired UAE national in 2025, rising to 9,000 AED per month (108,000 AED annually) in 2026 under Cabinet Resolution No. 19/M of 2022.
Do small companies with 20 to 49 employees have Emiratisation targets?
Yes, private establishments employing between 20 and 49 skilled workers within 14 designated economic sectors must hire at least one UAE citizen in 2024 and an additional citizen in 2025. Non-compliant firms face annual penalties of 96,000 AED in 2024 and 108,000 AED in 2025.
What is considered a skilled worker for MoHRE Emiratisation?
MoHRE defines skilled labor as personnel classified under professional levels 1 through 5: legislators, managers, business executives, professionals in technical and scientific fields, technicians, and specialized service personnel earning at least 4,000 AED monthly with accredited diplomas.
What are the legal consequences of fake Emiratisation (Tawteen Souri)?
Under Cabinet Resolution No. 44 of 2023, fraudulent Emiratisation incurs administrative fines ranging from 20,000 to 100,000 AED per fraudulent employee, immediate recovery of all NAFIS government financial subsidies, enterprise tier downgrading, and criminal prosecution for commercial fraud.
When are UAE Emiratisation penalties assessed and payable?
MoHRE assesses compliance semi-annually on June 30 (1% target) and December 31 (2% cumulative annual target). Invoices for financial contributions are generated electronically through the MoHRE digital portal in July and January, payable within thirty days.
