Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004: Article 54 Explained

In the State of Qatar, end-of-service gratuity is a statutory right protected under Law No. 14 of 2004 (Article 54) and monitored rigorously by the Ministry of Labour. Unlike discretionary retirement bonuses, this benefit is mandatory for all non-Qatari employees in the private sector upon concluding their employment contract.

Core Statutory Rules

  • Eligibility: The worker must complete at least one continuous year of employment with the company.
  • Minimum Accrual Rate: Not less than three weeks (21 days) of basic wage for each completed year of service.
  • Pro-Rata Fractions: The employee is entitled to gratuity for fractions of the year in proportion to the exact duration served.
  • Wage Definition: The calculation is based on the last drawn basic wage. Discretionary allowances or overtime do not inflate the gratuity baseline unless contractually specified.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the statutory minimum gratuity rate per year of service in Qatar?
Under Qatar Labour Law No. 14/2004 Article 54, end-of-service gratuity must not be less than three weeks (21 calendar days) of basic wage for each completed year of continuous service, prorated for partial years.
Are allowances included when calculating end-of-service benefits in Qatar?
Under Article 54 of Law No. 14/2004, end-of-service gratuity is calculated strictly on the employee last drawn basic wage. Regular allowances including accommodation, transport, and telephone subsidies are excluded from the statutory calculation base.
Does an employee lose end-of-service gratuity if they resign in Qatar?
Under Law No. 14/2004, employees who complete at least one year of continuous service retain full statutory gratuity rights upon resignation, provided statutory notice period requirements under Article 39 are strictly observed.
Under what circumstances can an employer in Qatar legally withhold gratuity?
Under Article 61 of Qatar Labour Law No. 14/2004, an employer may only dismiss a worker without end-of-service gratuity for severe statutory misconduct, such as gross financial loss, identity fraud, or physical assault.
What is the statutory timeframe for paying final dues under Qatar Labour Law?
Under Article 67 of Law No. 14/2004, employers must pay all wages and statutory severance benefits to the employee on the working day following the contract expiration or official termination date.
