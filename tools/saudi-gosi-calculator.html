1. The Statutory Architecture of Saudi Social Insurance

The General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI) administers contributory retirement, disability, and occupational protection across the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia under Royal Decree No. M/33 of 1421H. In the modern Saudi labour economy, compliance with GOSI contribution rules is electronically interconnected with the Qiwa labour platform, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD), and the Wage Protection System (WPS).

Every enterprise registered with the Ministry of Commerce must enroll eligible employees within statutory registration deadlines. A critical legal distinction exists between Saudi citizens and foreign expatriate workers: citizens participate in retirement annuity and unemployment pools, whereas expatriates participate solely in occupational hazards insurance.

2. Contribution Rates for Saudi National Employees: Two-Tier Statutory Framework

Following the enactment of Royal Decree No. M/273, the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia operates a two-tier social insurance contribution framework for national personnel, distinguishing between legacy contributors and new entrants:

  • Legacy Contributor Scheme (Royal Decree No. M/33): Applicable to Saudi citizens enrolled in GOSI prior to 3 July 2024. The Annuity branch requires 18.0% total (9.0% employee deduction and 9.0% employer liability). Adding 0.75% SANED unemployment insurance brings total employee payroll withholding to 9.75%. Employers remit 11.75% (9.0% Annuity + 0.75% SANED + 2.0% Occupational Hazards), representing a cumulative monthly contribution of 21.5%.
  • New Entrant Progressive Escalator (Royal Decree No. M/273): Applicable to workers entering the labour market on or after 3 July 2024. The Annuity rate increases incrementally by 0.5% each Gregorian year: 9.0% in 2024, 9.5% in 2025, reaching 10.0% during the active 2026/2027 cycle (resulting in 10.75% employee withholding and 12.75% employer liability, total 23.5%), rising to 10.5% in 2027, and capping at 11.0% (total 25.5%) from July 2028 onward.

3. Expatriate Worker Status: Zero Pension Withholding

A widespread point of confusion among multinational human resource departments concerns expatriate worker deductions. Under Article 4 of the Social Insurance Regulations, foreign workers residing in the Kingdom on work permits are exempt from the Annuities and SANED branches.

Employers are strictly prohibited from withholding any pension or retirement deductions from an expatriate's paycheck. The employer remains responsible for paying a flat 2.0% Occupational Hazard assessment directly to GOSI on behalf of the expatriate worker. This 2.0% cost is an employer tax, not an employee deduction.

4. Defining the Contributory Wage and the Statutory 45,000 SAR Cap

GOSI does not calculate percentages based on gross total compensation. Under GOSI regulations, the contributory wage comprises only two components:

  1. Basic Wage: The contractual monthly base wage recorded on Qiwa.
  2. Housing Allowance: Either the contractual cash housing allowance or, if housing is provided in kind, an assessed value equal to two months of basic wage divided by twelve.

Transportation allowances, commissions, performance bonuses, and overtime pay are legally excluded from GOSI contributory calculations. Additionally, GOSI imposes a strict statutory ceiling: any contributory wage exceeding 45,000 SAR per month is assessed only up to 45,000 SAR. Any salary amount above this cap is exempt from social insurance charges.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI) Compliance & Wage Inspection

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, the Social Insurance Law promulgated by Royal Decree No. M/22 and amended by Royal Decrees M/33 and M/273 governs compulsory pension, occupational hazard, and unemployment (SANED) protection. Under GOSI regulations, every commercial enterprise operating in the Kingdom must enroll all national and expatriate employees in the GOSI electronic portal within fifteen days of hiring.

Contributory wages are audited annually during the month of January. For legacy Saudi citizens, monthly contributions total 21.5% of the contributory wage (comprising basic salary and actual housing allowance), split into 9% annuity from the employee, 9% annuity from the employer, 0.75% SANED from the employer, 0.75% SANED from the employee, and 2% occupational hazards insurance paid exclusively by the employer.

For expatriate workers, the employer pays a mandatory 2% occupational hazard contribution to cover medical treatment, permanent disability stipends, and work injury compensation. Employers who deliberately misreport contributory wages or fail to remit monthly contributions face statutory fines of up to 10,000 SAR per unrecorded worker under Article 62 of the Social Insurance Law.

GOSI Pension Annuity Calculation & Expatriate Injury Claim Protocols

For Saudi citizens, retirement pension benefits under GOSI are calculated based on the average contributory wage during the final twenty-four months of service, multiplied by the number of contributory months and divided by statutory actuarial divisors. A minimum of 120 contributory months (or 60 months with special age exemptions) is required to qualify for retirement annuities upon reaching sixty Gregorian years of age.

In the event of an occupational accident or work-related illness affecting an expatriate employee, the employer must log the incident on the GOSI digital system within three business days. GOSI covers 100% of emergency medical care, rehabilitation, and statutory disability allowances through accredited healthcare networks across the Kingdom without deductibles for the foreign worker.

What are the mandatory GOSI contribution rates for Saudi national employees?
Under Royal Decree M/33, legacy Saudi nationals contribute 9.75% (9.0% Annuity plus 0.75% SANED) with employers paying 11.75% (including 2% hazards). Under Royal Decree M/273, new entrants face a progressive escalator reaching 10.75% employee deduction in 2026.
Do expatriate workers in Saudi Arabia pay GOSI retirement deductions?
Expatriate workers in Saudi Arabia are completely exempt from Annuity retirement deductions and SANED. Expat employees have zero percent (0%) withheld from their wages for social security retirement pensions.
What is the maximum monthly contributory wage ceiling under Saudi GOSI?
Under Saudi GOSI regulations, the maximum wage subject to social insurance contributions is capped at SAR 45,000 per month, consisting of monthly basic wage plus registered contractual housing allowances.
Who pays the 2 percent GOSI Occupational Hazard contribution in Saudi Arabia?
Under Royal Decree No. M/22, the 2% Occupational Hazards contribution is paid 100% by the employing enterprise for both Saudi nationals and expatriate workers, with zero deductions from the worker salary.
What benefits does the GOSI Occupational Hazard branch provide to injured workers?
The GOSI Occupational Hazard branch provides 100% free comprehensive emergency medical treatment, daily injury allowances, permanent disability pensions, and statutory survivor death benefits for on-the-job accidents.
