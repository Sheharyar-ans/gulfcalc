Saudi Labour Law Articles 84, 85, and 87: The Definitive Guide

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, terminal severance pay is codified under the Saudi Labour Law (Royal Decree No. M/51) as the End of Service Award (Mukafoat Nihayat Al-Khidma). Governed by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD), the award represents a statutory obligation upon the employer at the conclusion of the contractual relationship.

The Mandatory 'Actual Wage' Calculation Basis

A widespread compliance error among private companies is computing the award exclusively on basic salary. Article 2 of the Saudi Labour Law defines the wage for terminal benefits as the Actual Wage (Al-Ajr Al-Fi'li), which includes:

  • Monthly Basic Salary;
  • Fixed Housing Allowance (or fair cash value of company-provided housing);
  • Fixed Transportation Allowance;
  • Any recurring, fixed allowances contractually paid without condition.
Saudi End of Service Accrual Formula (Article 84)
Actual Wage = Basic Salary + Housing Allowance + Transport Allowance
Years 1 to 5: Accrual = (Actual Wage / 2) × Years
Year 6 Onwards: Accrual = Actual Wage × (Years - 5)
Gross Award = Accrual (1-5 yrs) + Accrual (6+ yrs)

Statutory Resignation Deduction Scale (Article 85)

When an employee resigns under an indefinite term employment contract, Article 85 imposes a structured deduction scale based on completed service:

Continuous Service Period Entitlement Percentage Statutory Reduction
Less than 2 Completed Years 0% 100% Forfeited
2 Years to less than 5 Years 33.33% (One-Third) 66.67% Deducted
5 Years to less than 10 Years 66.67% (Two-Thirds) 33.33% Deducted
10 or more Completed Years 100% (Full Award) Zero Deduction

Article 87 Full Award Exceptions

Notwithstanding Article 85, Article 87 dictates that an employee is entitled to 100% full award upon resignation if:

  • The female employee leaves work within six (6) months of marriage;
  • The female employee leaves work within three (3) months of giving birth;
  • Separation is due to force majeure beyond the worker's control;
  • The worker terminates the contract under Article 81 due to employer breach of contractual obligations or assault.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Saudi end-of-service calculated on basic salary or total salary?
Under Saudi Royal Decree No. M/51 Article 84, end-of-service benefits are calculated on actual wage, which encompasses monthly basic salary plus all regular, contractual cash allowances including housing and fixed transportation stipends.
What percentage of gratuity does an employee receive when resigning in Saudi Arabia?
Under Article 85 of Saudi Labour Law, resignation yields zero percent for service under 2 years, one-third (33.33%) for 2 to 5 years, two-thirds (66.67%) for 5 to 10 years, and full 100% entitlement after 10 completed years.
When is a resigning worker entitled to full 100 percent gratuity in Saudi Arabia?
Under Article 87 of Royal Decree No. M/51, employees receive 100% full gratuity upon resignation due to force majeure, or female workers resigning within 6 months of marriage or 3 months of giving birth.
What is the legal deadline for a Saudi employer to pay terminal dues?
Under Article 88 of Saudi Labour Law, employers must settle final compensation within one week if the employer terminates employment, or within two weeks if the employee terminates the contract via voluntary resignation.
Can an employer in Saudi Arabia make deductions from end-of-service awards?
Under Article 92 of Royal Decree No. M/51, employers may only deduct legally documented debts, authorized salary advances, or court-ordered garnishments, provided statutory deduction percentages are not exceeded without written labor ministry authorization.
