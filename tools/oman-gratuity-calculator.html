Oman Labour Law Reform: The Transition from RD 35/2003 to RD 53/2023

On July 25, 2023, His Majesty Sultan Haitham bin Tarik issued Royal Decree No. 53/2023, promulgating the new Labour Law of the Sultanate of Oman and officially repealing the longstanding Royal Decree No. 35/2003. This legislative overhaul introduced profound improvements for expatriate workers, particularly regarding terminal severance accrual.

The New Flat 30-Day Accrual Standard

Under the old framework (RD 35/2003), non-Omani employees received 15 days of basic wage per year for the first three continuous years of service, and 30 days per year for each subsequent year.

Under Royal Decree 53/2023 (Article 61), this bifurcation has been eliminated. Expatriate employees now accrue a flat one full month's basic salary (30 days' wage) for each completed year from year one. Fractions of a year are calculated pro-rata on the basis of the final basic wage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Ministry of Labour Governance & Royal Decree No. 53 of 2023 Enforcement

The Sultanate of Oman significantly enhanced expatriate labor rights with the promulgation of Royal Decree No. 53 of 2023, issuing the new Oman Labour Law. Under Article 61, non-Omani workers are entitled to an end-of-service gratuity calculated on the basis of their final basic monthly wage at the rate of one full month's basic salary (30 days) for each completed year of service, starting from the first year.

The Ministry of Labour enforces compliance through digital employment contracts registered on the MoL online portal. Under Article 63, all end-of-service benefits, accrued leave compensations, and return ticket allowances must be settled within seven working days from the date employment concludes.

In cases of non-payment, employees can lodge a formal grievance on the Ministry of Labour digital portal. The ministry conducts mandatory mediation sessions. If the grievance remains unresolved within twenty-one days, it is referred to the competent court of first instance, where legal proceedings are exempt from judicial fees for employees.

Social Protection Law (Royal Decree 52/2023) Transition & Expat Fund

Oman's new Social Protection Law, enacted under Royal Decree No. 52 of 2023, establishes a transformative framework for expatriate retirement and severance security. The law envisions transitioning traditional employer-held book-reserve gratuity provisions into a centralized Social Protection Fund (SPF).

Once fully implemented for the expatriate workforce, employers will pay monthly contributions into individual worker accounts managed by the state fund. This structural reform eliminates the financial risk of unpaid severance resulting from private corporate bankruptcies.

Terminal payouts in Oman are completely exempt from personal income tax under Tax Authority regulations. Workers converting Omani Rials (OMR) for outward remittance enjoy peg stability to the US Dollar (fixed at 1 OMR = 2.6008 USD), facilitating cost-effective global repatriation.

Ministry of Labour Wage Protection System (WPS) & Termination Audits

In the Sultanate of Oman, the Ministry of Labour (MoL) enforces the mandatory Wage Protection System (WPS) in partnership with the Central Bank of Oman (CBO). Under Article 87 of Royal Decree No. 53 of 2023, private commercial enterprises must process all monthly employee wages and final end-of-service settlements through authorized Omani commercial bank accounts.

When an employee's service terminates, the employer must upload the final clearance certificate (Bara'at Dhimma) and severance calculation sheet to the MoL electronic portal. Non-compliant establishments that fail to disburse severance entitlements within seven working days incur administrative suspension of corporate labor clearances, prohibiting the entity from hiring foreign workers or renewing investor visas.

Workers with disputed severance calculations can submit an electronic claim to the Labour Disputes Department. The department facilitates mandatory settlement hearings within twenty-one days, providing non-Omani workers with an expedited, cost-free legal avenue to recover accrued benefits under the law.

In addition, expatriate professionals completing service contracts in the Sultanate should ensure all outstanding annual leave allowances are computed at the full gross rate rather than basic wage alone under Article 65 of the Labour Law. Liquidating accrued leave balances alongside the statutory 30-day severance accrual guarantees complete financial settlement prior to the cancellation of resident work visas.

How did Royal Decree 53/2023 change end-of-service calculations in Oman?
Under Article 61 of Oman Royal Decree No. 53/2023, gratuity accrues at a flat rate of one full month basic wage (30 days) per year from year one onwards, completely replacing the former split formula of 15 days.
Is Oman gratuity calculated on basic salary or gross remuneration?
Under Article 61 of Royal Decree No. 53/2023, gratuity computation is determined strictly by the employee last drawn basic wage. Supplemental allowances such as housing, utility, and conveyance allowances are legally excluded.
What is the minimum service period required to qualify for gratuity in Oman?
Under Article 61 of Oman Labour Law, expatriate employees must complete at least one continuous year of employment with the employer to qualify for statutory end-of-service gratuity entitlements.
Does an expatriate worker in Oman lose gratuity rights upon voluntary resignation?
Under Royal Decree No. 53/2023, expatriate employees who complete one continuous year of service retain full gratuity entitlements upon resignation, provided they comply with statutory notice obligations specified in Article 43.
How does the Oman Social Protection Fund impact expatriate gratuity payouts?
Under Oman Royal Decree No. 52/2023 (Social Protection Law), private enterprises are transitioning toward monthly savings contributions administered by the state fund, modernizing terminal benefit security for foreign nationals across the Sultanate.
