Saudi Labour Law Articles 84 & 85: The Comprehensive Guide to Actual Wage and Resignation Tiers

Under Saudi Labour Law (Royal Decree No. M/51), Article 84 awards 15 days actual wage per year for the first 5 years and 30 days thereafter. Voluntary resignation under Article 85 reduces payouts: zero below 2 years, one-third for 2 to 5 years, two-thirds for 5 to 10 years, and 100% after 10 years.
Published by GulfCalc Compliance Specialists • Statute: Saudi Royal Decree No. M/51

1. Statutory Context of the Saudi End of Service Award

In the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, terminal severance compensation is formally designated as the End of Service Award (Mukafoat Nihayat Al-Khidma). Governed by Royal Decree No. M/51 (as amended) and enforced by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD), the award represents a non-negotiable statutory obligation owed by the employer to the employee upon the conclusion of their employment relationship.

Navigating Saudi severance entitlements requires an exact understanding of two interconnected legal concepts: the definition of the statutory wage baseline under Article 2, and the application of tenure deduction scales under Article 85 when employment terminates via employee resignation.

2. The Mandatory 'Actual Wage' Calculation Baseline (Article 2)

The most prevalent source of labour dispute in Saudi Arabia stems from employers attempting to compute severance pay exclusively using an employee's basic salary. Under Article 2 of the Saudi Labour Law, this practice is strictly unlawful.

The law differentiates between Basic Wage (Al-Ajr Al-Asasi) and Actual Wage (Al-Ajr Al-Fi'li):

  • Basic Wage: The core contracted remuneration agreed between the parties, excluding all allowances.
  • Actual Wage: The Basic Wage plus all regular, recurring cash allowances provided to the employee. This explicitly includes mandatory housing allowances (or the cash equivalent of company-provided accommodations) and regular transportation allowances.

Because Article 84 specifies that the award must be calculated on the basis of the worker's latest drawn comprehensive wage, any corporate policy that calculates gratuity solely on basic pay violates public policy. Under Article 8 of the Labour Law, any agreement that waives or reduces statutory worker rights is legally void.

Saudi Actual Wage Composition (Article 2)
Actual Wage = Basic Salary + Fixed Housing Allowance + Fixed Transportation Allowance
Daily Wage = Actual Wage / 30

3. Accrual Rates Under Article 84

When an employment contract concludes due to natural expiration, mutual separation, or employer-initiated termination without misconduct, Article 84 establishes the baseline accrual framework:

  • Years 1 Through 5: Half of one month's Actual Wage for each completed year of service (equivalent to fifteen days of wage per year).
  • Year 6 and Beyond: One full month's Actual Wage for each subsequent completed year of service (equivalent to thirty days of wage per year).
  • Fractions of a Year: The employee is entitled to an exact pro-rata proportion for completed months and days.

4. Resignation Deduction Tiers Under Article 85

Unlike the United Arab Emirates, where resignation and termination have been unified, Saudi Labour Law maintains distinct statutory deduction tiers if an employee voluntarily resigns from an indefinite term employment contract.

Article 85 governs these reductions:

Continuous Service Tenure Entitlement Percentage Statutory Forfeiture Legal Rationale
Under 2 Completed Years 0% 100% Forfeited Tenure below statutory qualification threshold
2 Years to less than 5 Years 33.33% (One-Third) 66.67% Deducted Partial vesting under Article 85
5 Years to less than 10 Years 66.67% (Two-Thirds) 33.33% Deducted Substantial vesting under Article 85
10 or more Completed Years 100% (Full Award) 0% Deducted Full severance vesting achieved

5. Statutory Exceptions Under Article 87 (Full Award on Resignation)

An essential nuance of the Saudi regime is Article 87, which overrides Article 85 under specific social and emergency conditions. Under Article 87, an employee who resigns voluntarily is entitled to their full, unreduced award regardless of their tenure if:

  • The female worker resigns within six (6) months from the date of her marriage;
  • The female worker resigns within three (3) months from the date of giving birth;
  • The worker leaves employment as a result of proven force majeure events beyond their control;
  • The worker resigns under Article 81 due to an employer's material default, such as non-payment of wages, contractual breach, or physical assault.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Saudi end-of-service calculated on basic salary or total actual salary?
Under Article 84 of the Saudi Labour Law (Royal Decree No. M/51), end-of-service award calculations must be based on the worker's 'actual wage'. As interpreted by the Ministry of Human Resources, this includes basic salary plus fixed contractual housing and transportation allowances.
How much gratuity does an employee receive if they resign before 5 years in Saudi Arabia?
Under Article 85 of Royal Decree No. M/51, an employee who resigns with less than two years of service receives zero award. If tenure is between two and five continuous years, the worker is entitled to exactly one-third (33.33%) of the standard accrued award.
What are the statutory exceptions where full gratuity is paid on resignation under Article 87?
Under Article 87 of the Saudi Labour Law, full 100% gratuity is payable upon resignation if a female worker resigns within six months of marriage or three months of childbirth, or if the worker leaves due to force majeure or employer contractual default under Article 81.
Can an employer legally withhold an employee's passport in Saudi Arabia?
No, retaining a worker's passport is strictly illegal in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Under Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development regulations, employers face automated administrative fines of 2,000 SAR for each withheld passport.
What is the legal deadline for paying end-of-service award in Saudi Arabia?
Under Article 88 of the Saudi Labour Law, if the employment contract is terminated by the employer, settlement must be paid within one week. If the employee tenders their resignation, the employer is granted a maximum statutory window of two weeks to disburse final dues.
