UAE Gratuity Calculation Under Limited Contracts in 2026: The Definitive Legal Analysis

Under UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 (Article 51), private sector workers on unified limited contracts earn 21 days basic salary per year for the first 5 years and 30 days per year thereafter, with zero resignation penalties and a 2-year salary ceiling.
Published by GulfCalc Legal Research Team • Statute: UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021

1. The Legislative Overhaul of UAE Employment Contracts

Prior to February 2022, private-sector employment across the United Arab Emirates operated under Federal Law No. 8 of 1980. That statutory framework divided workers into two fundamentally distinct contractual categories: limited (fixed-term) contracts and unlimited contracts. The calculations governing end-of-service gratuity under the 1980 regime were notoriously complex, unpredictable, and punitive toward workers who chose to resign voluntarily before completing specified milestones.

With the enforcement of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 (the UAE Labour Law), documented in our GCC Labour Laws Directory,, the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) initiated a complete harmonisation of the national workforce. Unlimited contracts were formally phased out. All private-sector employees on the UAE mainland and non-financial free zones must now be engaged under fixed-term (limited) contracts, renewable upon mutual consent.

The most significant financial consequence of this reform is the total elimination of voluntary resignation deductions. Under Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, an employee who resigns voluntarily after completing at least one continuous year of employment receives exactly the same statutory gratuity as an employee terminated without cause by their employer.

2. Statutory Accrual Rates Under Article 51

The mathematical calculation on our UAE Gratuity Calculator is anchored strictly to the worker's latest drawn basic salary. Fixed allowances, such as cash paid for housing, utilities, child education, or transportation, are excluded from the statutory severance baseline.

Article 51, Clause 2 outlines the two statutory accrual tiers:

  • Service Period from Year 1 Through Year 5: Twenty-one (21) calendar days of basic wage for each completed year of service.
  • Service Period Beyond Five Years: Thirty (30) calendar days of basic wage for each subsequent completed year of service.
  • Pro-Rata Partial Service: For partial years completed after the initial one-year qualification threshold, the employee is entitled to a proportionate fraction calculated to the exact day.
UAE Statutory Gratuity Formula (Article 51)
Daily Wage = Monthly Basic Salary / 30
Years 1 to 5 Accrual = Daily Wage × 21 × Completed Years (up to 5)
Year 6+ Accrual = Daily Wage × 30 × (Total Completed Years - 5)
Gross Gratuity = Tier 1 Accrual + Tier 2 Accrual

4. The Statutory Maximum Cap (Article 51, Clause 3)

While the accrual rates are generous, the UAE Labour Law imposes a strict statutory ceiling to cap corporate liabilities. Article 51, Clause 3 specifies that the total aggregate end-of-service gratuity payable to any worker shall not exceed two years' basic salary (twenty-four months of basic wage).

For long-tenured employees, this cap represents a definitive cutoff. For instance, an executive earning a monthly basic salary of 30,000 AED has a statutory maximum ceiling of 720,000 AED (30,000 × 24). Even if mathematical formulas indicate an uncapped figure of 850,000 AED after twenty-four years of service, the legal entitlement cannot exceed 720,000 AED.

6. Disbursal Deadlines and Employer Penalties

In addition to standardizing calculation formulas, Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 introduced stringent compliance mechanisms regarding payment timelines. Under Article 53, an employer is legally obligated to settle all outstanding employee wages, untaken annual leave compensations, and end-of-service gratuities within fourteen (14) days from the formal contract termination date.

Failure to settle within this statutory window permits the employee to lodge an expedited formal complaint with MOHRE. Unresolved disputes exceeding 50,000 AED are referred directly to the competent labour court, while claims below 50,000 AED are subject to summary binding decisions issued directly by MOHRE adjudicators.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is UAE gratuity calculated under limited employment contracts in 2026?
Under Article 51 of UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, end-of-service gratuity is calculated at 21 calendar days of basic salary for each of the first five years of service, and 30 calendar days for each subsequent year, capped at two full years of basic salary.
Does an employee forfeit gratuity if they resign in the UAE?
No, under the unified employment framework of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, employees who complete at least one year of continuous service receive 100% of their statutory gratuity entitlement upon resignation. The historical reduction deductions for resignation were permanently abolished.
What is the maximum statutory cap on UAE gratuity?
Under Article 51(5) of UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, the maximum aggregate gratuity payout cannot exceed two full years of the employee's final basic wage (twenty-four months of basic salary), regardless of how many decades the worker remained in service.
Can an employer deduct visa or onboarding costs from final gratuity?
No, under Article 6(4) of UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, the employer is legally obligated to bear all recruitment, licensing, and visa processing fees. Deducting visa or onboarding costs from an employee's final gratuity settlement is strictly illegal under UAE federal law.
How quickly must an employer pay the final settlement in the UAE?
Under Article 53 of UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, the employer must settle all outstanding wages, unutilized annual leave encashment, and end-of-service gratuity within fourteen (14) calendar days from the employee's formal contract termination date.
