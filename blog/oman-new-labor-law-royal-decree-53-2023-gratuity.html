Oman Labour Law Royal Decree 53/2023: The Modern 30-Day Gratuity Standard

Under Oman Royal Decree No. 53 of 2023 (Article 61), non-Omani expatriate employees earn a flat 30 days basic wage for every completed year of continuous service starting from year one, permanently replacing the historical two-tier 15-day scale of repealed RD 35/2003.
Published by GulfCalc Regional Legal Team • Statute: Oman Royal Decree No. 53/2023

1. The Promulgation of Royal Decree No. 53/2023

On July 25, 2023, His Majesty Sultan Haitham bin Tarik issued Royal Decree No. 53/2023, officially promulgating the new Labour Law of the Sultanate of Oman. Published in Official Gazette Issue No. 1504 and taking effect on July 31, 2023, this comprehensive enactment permanently repealed Royal Decree No. 35/2003, which had regulated private-sector employment relations for over two decades.

The primary objective of the 2023 Labour Law was to balance nationalisation priorities (Omanisation) with modern, internationally aligned worker protections designed to attract high-caliber foreign expertise. Among the most impactful reforms for non-Omani personnel was the total restructuring of Article 61, which governs end of service gratuity.

2. The Modern Flat 30-Day Accrual Standard

Under the historical framework of the repealed Royal Decree No. 35/2003, expatriate employees faced a bifurcated accrual formula:

  • Fifteen (15) days of basic wage for each of the first three continuous years of service;
  • Thirty (30) days of basic wage for each subsequent completed year of service.

Under Article 61 of Royal Decree No. 53/2023, this two-tier system was abolished. Non-Omani employees who are not enrolled in the national Social Protection Fund now accrue a flat one full month's basic wage (thirty days) for every completed year of service, beginning from their very first day of employment.

Oman Royal Decree 53/2023 Gratuity Formula (Article 61)
Daily Wage = Monthly Basic Wage / 30
Gratuity = Monthly Basic Wage × (Completed Years + [Completed Months / 12])

3. Detailed Comparison: RD 35/2003 vs RD 53/2023

The reform provides an immediate, substantial financial benefit to early-tenure expatriate employees in the Sultanate. The following comparative matrix demonstrates the accrued entitlement across varying service milestones.

4. Transitional Mechanics for Historical Service

A critical legal inquiry for human resource directors and in-house legal counsel concerns tenures that straddle the July 31, 2023 enactment date. In corporate practice and civil court adjudication, two administrative methodologies are observed:

  • Unified Post-Decree Standard: Many progressive multinational enterprises in Oman elected to calculate the full tenure under the modern 30-day rule to avoid complex dual calculations.
  • Bifurcated Pro-Rata Transition: Where an employer elects strict statutory preservation, periods completed prior to July 31, 2023 are accrued under the 15-day/30-day tiers of RD 35/2003, with all service rendered after July 31, 2023 accruing under the flat 30-day rule of RD 53/2023.

5. Absence of Resignation Penalty Tiers

Unlike Kuwait (Article 53) and Saudi Arabia (Article 85), the Oman Labour Law does not enforce statutory percentage forfeiture tables for employees who resign voluntarily. Provided the non-Omani employee serves their contractual notice period (minimum thirty days for monthly paid personnel under Article 43), they are legally entitled to receive their full end-of-service gratuity upon departure.

6. Wage Base Definition Under Article 1

Article 1 of Royal Decree No. 53/2023 explicitly defines Basic Wage (Al-Ajr Al-Asasi) as the remuneration agreed between the employer and worker in return for work performed, excluding all allowances regardless of their nature. Consequently, housing stipends, transport allowances, and telephone subsidies are legally excluded from the gratuity base in Oman, contrasting sharply with the Saudi Actual Wage requirement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the gratuity rate under the new Oman Labour Law Royal Decree 53/2023?
Under Article 61 of Oman Labour Law promulgated by Royal Decree No. 53/2023, non-Omani expatriate employees are entitled to a flat one full month's basic salary (thirty days' wage) for every completed year of service, starting from the first year of employment.
How does Oman calculate gratuity for service spanning before and after July 2023?
Service completed prior to July 25, 2023 is calculated under repealed Royal Decree 35/2003 (15 days per year for the first three years, 30 days thereafter). Service completed after July 25, 2023 is calculated under Royal Decree 53/2023 at 30 days per year.
Does an employee forfeit gratuity upon resignation in Oman?
No, under Royal Decree No. 53/2023, expatriate workers who resign after completing at least one continuous year of service receive their full statutory end-of-service gratuity, provided they serve their contractual thirty-day notice period under Article 43.
What salary components are used to calculate gratuity in Oman?
Under Article 61 of Royal Decree No. 53/2023, end-of-service gratuity is calculated on the basis of the employee's last drawn basic wage, explicitly excluding housing allowances, transport allowances, overtime compensation, and variable bonuses.
How does the Social Protection Law affect expatriate terminal benefits in Oman?
Under Royal Decree No. 52/2023 (Social Protection Law), Oman is phasing in a centralized National Provident Fund administered by the Social Protection Fund. Once fully operational for expatriates, employer monthly contributions will replace final balance-sheet gratuity reserves.
