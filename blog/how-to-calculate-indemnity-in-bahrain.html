How to Calculate Indemnity in Bahrain: SIO Expat Rules, Article 116 Formula, and Resignation Guidelines

Under Article 116 of Bahrain Law No. 36 of 2012, private sector workers receive half a month basic wage per year for the first three years of service, and one full month basic wage per year thereafter, with recent SIO reforms establishing direct monthly contributions.
Published by GulfCalc Financial & Labour Law Editorial Team • October 31, 2026 • 7 min read • Jurisdiction: Kingdom of Bahrain (Law No. 36 of 2012)

End of service indemnity represents the core statutory severance entitlement for expatriates in the Kingdom of Bahrain. Codified under Article 116 of the Bahrain Labour Law for the Private Sector (Law No. 36 of 2012) and indexed in our GCC Labour Laws Directory, this scheme provides deferred monetary compensation upon employment separation. Whether departing through contract completion, termination, or resignation, calculating statutory severance accurately is essential for payroll governance.

Bahrain overhauled its terminal benefits structure through Social Insurance Organisation (SIO) reforms under Ministerial Decision No. 109 of 2023, transitioning private firms from internal balance-sheet accruals to a centralized state-backed deposit system. To compute entitlements under both pre-reform service accruals and current SIO rates, professionals can utilize our verified Bahrain EOSI and VAT calculator.

Statutory Calculation Formula Under Article 116

Article 116 of Law No. 36 of 2012 defines the foundational mathematical mechanism for computing terminal gratuity in Bahrain. The statute applies to all workers who are not covered under the provisions of the Social Insurance Law governing old-age pensions for Bahraini nationals. Consequently, expatriate professionals represent the primary demographic entitled to cash indemnity under this article.

The legal formula operates on a graduated tenure structure. Unlike regional peers such as the UAE, which applies a flat 21-day annual rate for the initial five years, or jurisdictions evaluated in our Kuwait indemnity calculator, Bahrain splits the statutory rate at the three-year mark.

Breaking Down the First Three Years vs. Subsequent Service Years

The calculation tiers established under Article 116 are structured as follows:

Statutory Accrual Rates (Law No. 36 of 2012, Article 116)
  • Initial Service Tier (Years 1 to 3): One-half of a month basic wage (15 calendar days) for each completed year of continuous employment.
  • Subsequent Service Tier (Year 4 Onward): One full month basic wage (30 calendar days) for each completed year of employment beyond the third year.
  • Fractional Service Pro-Rata: Employees who complete fractions of a year receive proportional indemnity calculated on a pro-rata basis for exact days and months served.

A fundamental principle under Bahraini jurisprudence is that indemnity is computed strictly against the employee last drawn basic wage. Ministerial Decrees governing commercial employment define basic wage as the base contractual remuneration agreed upon between the parties, excluding variable housing allowances, company transportation stipends, telephone allowances, performance commissions, or overtime pay. If an employer increases an employee basic wage during their tenure, the final indemnity calculation applies the highest recent basic salary rate across the entire duration of service.

The SIO Expatriate End-of-Service Reform: What Changed for Foreign Employees

Prior to March 2024, private employers in Bahrain managed indemnity reserves as unfunded accounting provisions on company balance sheets. In the event of company insolvency, commercial restructuring, or sudden financial distress, expatriates frequently faced lengthy disputes before the Labour Case Administration Office to recover unpaid severance packages.

To permanently protect expatriate entitlements, Bahrain implemented Law No. 14 of 2022 and Ministerial Decision No. 109 of 2023. This regulatory framework mandated that employers transition from internal bookkeeping accruals to direct monthly financial deposits into the Social Insurance Organisation (SIO).

Under the SIO regime, the employer remits a monthly percentage derived mathematically from Article 116:

  • Months 1 through 36 (Years 1 to 3): 4.2% of the monthly basic wage (reflecting half a month salary spread across 12 calendar months).
  • Month 37 and beyond (Year 4 Onward): 8.4% of the monthly basic wage (reflecting one full month salary spread across 12 calendar months).

These monthly contributions are held in a ring-fenced national fund administered by the SIO. When an expatriate resigns or is terminated, the employer finalizes the separation file through the Labour Market Regulatory Authority (LMRA) portal, and the SIO releases the accumulated cash balance directly into the worker verified international bank account (IBAN).

Resignation vs. Termination Rules for Indefinite and Fixed-Term Contracts

A frequent question among expatriates is whether voluntary resignation diminishes their indemnity entitlement. In several neighboring Gulf countries, such as those analyzed in our Saudi end of service calculator, statutory law imposes substantial reductions if an employee resigns during the first five years of service.

In the Kingdom of Bahrain, the legal position is substantially more favorable to employees. Under Article 116, the right to end of service indemnity is linked to the termination of the employment contract, without penalizing voluntary resignation. An expatriate who resigns voluntarily retains 100% of their statutory indemnity entitlement, provided they comply with contractual and statutory notice obligations.

Contractual notice requirements differ depending on whether the contract is indefinite or fixed-term:

  • Indefinite Contracts (Article 99): Either party may terminate the contract by providing at least 30 calendar days written notice. If an employee resigns and serves this statutory period, no indemnity reduction can be enforced. If the employee leaves without serving notice, the employer may deduct compensation in lieu of notice from the final settlement.
  • Fixed-Term Contracts (Article 106): If an employee resigns prior to the expiry of a fixed-term contract without cause, the employee may be liable to pay statutory compensation not exceeding three months wages or the remaining contract duration, whichever is less, unless the contract specifies otherwise. However, accrued indemnity for past completed years remains the employee property.

Deductions, Final Settlements, and Unused Annual Leave Encashment in Bahrain

The end of service settlement in Bahrain is not restricted to indemnity alone. Human resources departments must assemble an all-inclusive exit statement incorporating several statutory components:

  1. Accrued Statutory Indemnity: Calculated under Article 116 or disbursed through the SIO repository.
  2. Unused Annual Leave Encashment: Under Article 58, private sector workers are entitled to 30 days of paid annual leave per year (2.5 days per month). Any leave balance remaining at separation must be paid in cash based on the employee full last-drawn wage (including regular basic wage and fixed monthly allowances).
  3. Repatriation Airfare: Under Article 27 of the Bahrain Labour Law and LMRA guidelines, the employer must bear the cost of an economy-class return airfare ticket to the worker home country, unless the employee enters new sponsorship in Bahrain within the statutory transfer window.
  4. Statutory Wage Balances: Pro-rata compensation for days worked in the final calendar month up to the official date of release.

Under Article 25, an employer may not make arbitrary deductions from the employee terminal settlement. Legal deductions are strictly limited to outstanding salary advances, verified company loans, authorized judicial attachments, or documented damages resulting from proven gross negligence under Article 107.

Step-by-Step Calculation Case Study: An Expatriate with 6 Years of Continuous Service

To demonstrate Article 116 in practice, consider an empirical calculation for an expatriate professional completing service in Manama.

Case Profile: Senior Systems Architect
  • Total Continuous Service: 6 years and 4 months (6.333 years).
  • Last Drawn Basic Salary: 1,200 Bahraini Dinars (BHD) per month.
  • Fixed Allowances: 350 BHD (Housing) + 150 BHD (Transport) = 500 BHD (Excluded from indemnity).
  • Total Monthly Compensation: 1,700 BHD.
  • Unused Annual Leave: 15 accrued working days.
Step 1: Compute Initial Tier (Years 1 through 3)

For the first 3 years of service, the statutory rate is 0.5 months basic wage per completed year:
ext{Tier 1 Accrual} = 3 ext{ Years} imes (1,200 ext{ BHD} imes 0.5) = 3 imes 600 ext{ BHD} = 1,800 ext{ BHD}.

Step 2: Compute Subsequent Tier (Remaining 3 Years and 4 Months)

For service exceeding 3 years, the statutory rate is 1.0 month basic wage per completed year, plus pro-rata for fractional months:
ext{Full Completed Years (Years 4, 5, 6)} = 3 ext{ Years} imes 1,200 ext{ BHD} = 3,600 ext{ BHD}.
ext{Fractional 4 Months} = rac{4}{12} imes 1,200 ext{ BHD} = 400 ext{ BHD}.
ext{Tier 2 Accrual} = 3,600 ext{ BHD} + 400 ext{ BHD} = 4,000 ext{ BHD}.

Step 3: Total Statutory Indemnity

ext{Total Indemnity} = 1,800 ext{ BHD (Tier 1)} + 4,000 ext{ BHD (Tier 2)} = 5,800 ext{ BHD}.

Step 4: Unused Annual Leave Cash Encashment

Leave encashment must be based on total gross remuneration (1,700 BHD):
ext{Daily Gross Rate} = rac{1,700 ext{ BHD}}{30 ext{ Days}} = 56.67 ext{ BHD/Day}.
ext{Leave Payment} = 15 ext{ Days} imes 56.67 ext{ BHD} = 850 ext{ BHD}.
Total Comprehensive Exit Settlement: 5,800 BHD + 850 BHD = 6,650 BHD.

Frequently Asked Questions

What salary figure is used to calculate end of service indemnity in Bahrain?

Under Article 116 of Law No. 36 of 2012, end of service indemnity is calculated exclusively on the worker last drawn basic wage. Regular allowances such as housing, transportation, utility stipends, and discretionary bonuses are excluded from the statutory indemnity wage base unless explicitly stipulated otherwise in the written employment contract.

Does an employee lose their indemnity if they resign voluntarily in Bahrain?

No. Under Bahrain Labour Law, voluntary resignation does not forfeit end of service indemnity. Unlike some jurisdictions that apply penalty deduction tiers during early resignation, an expatriate worker in Bahrain who completes valid statutory notice retains their full accrued indemnity under Article 116.

How did the 2024 SIO reform change expatriate indemnity payouts?

Ministerial Decision No. 109 of 2023 required private employers to stop holding severance accruals as internal accounting provisions. Since March 2024, employers remit monthly deposits directly to the Social Insurance Organisation (4.2% for the first three years, and 8.4% thereafter). The SIO holds these funds in trust and disburses them directly to the expatriate bank account upon visa cancellation.

How are fractional years of employment calculated under Article 116?

Article 116 mandates that an employee is entitled to indemnity for fractions of a year in proportion to the exact duration of completed service. Days and months worked beyond complete years are calculated on a pro-rata basis using the statutory daily rate of the applicable service tier.

What deductions can an employer legally make from the final indemnity settlement?

Under Article 25 of Law No. 36 of 2012, employers may only deduct documented employee debts, unreturned company property, salary advances, court-ordered judgments, or notice period shortfalls. Deductions cannot exceed statutory limits, and any disputed debt must be documented through payroll receipts or written employee consent.
