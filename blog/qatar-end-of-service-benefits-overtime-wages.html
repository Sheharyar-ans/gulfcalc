Qatar End of Service Benefits and Statutory Overtime Under Law No. 14 of 2004

Under Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004 (Article 54), expatriate workers who complete at least one continuous year earn end-of-service benefits calculated at a minimum of 21 calendar days basic wage per completed year, plus exact pro-rata fractions for partial service.
Published by GulfCalc Legal Research Team • Statute: Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004

1. Statutory Framework of Private Sector Employment in Qatar

In the State of Qatar, private-sector employment is regulated by Law No. 14 of 2004 (the Labour Law) as amended by subsequent ministerial decrees. Supported by the Ministry of Labour, Qatar has enacted landmark reforms, including the historic introduction of Law No. 17 of 2020, which established a non-discriminatory national minimum wage and dismantled the historical requirement for employer exit permits.

Two aspects of payroll administration require strict adherence to prevent compliance penalties: the calculation of end-of-service gratuity under Article 54, and the computation of statutory overtime wages under Articles 74 and 75.

2. Article 54 End of Service Gratuity Rules

Under Article 54 of Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004, the employer is legally obligated to remit end-of-service benefits to any worker who completes at least one continuous year of employment. The statute defines clear parameters:

  • Statutory Minimum Rate: The benefit agreed between employer and employee cannot be less than three weeks (twenty-one calendar days) of basic wage for each completed year of service.
  • Wage Definition: The calculation is benchmarked exclusively on the worker's latest drawn basic salary. Overtime, travel stipends, and discretionary allowances are excluded.
  • Fractional Accrual: The employee is entitled to compensation for fractions of a year in exact proportion to the period served, down to partial months and days.
  • Resignation Treatment: Once an employee completes one continuous year of service, voluntary resignation does not trigger percentage deductions under Qatar law.

Daily Basic Wage = Monthly Basic Salary / 30
Statutory Gratuity = Daily Basic Wage × 21 × (Completed Years + [Completed Months / 12])

3. Statutory Overtime Wage Computations (Articles 74 & 75)

Standard working hours in Qatar are established under Article 73 at maximum eight (8) hours per day, or forty-eight (48) hours per week during normal months (reduced to thirty-six hours per week during the Holy Month of Ramadan). Any labor performed beyond statutory standard hours is classified as overtime and governed by Articles 74 and 75:

Daytime Overtime (Article 74)

For additional work performed during normal daytime hours (between 6:00 AM and 9:00 PM), the worker is entitled to their basic wage for the extra hours plus an overtime premium of not less than twenty-five percent (25%) of that basic wage.

Nighttime Overtime and Rest Day Labor (Article 75)

For work performed between 9:00 PM and 6:00 AM, the statutory premium increases to not less than fifty percent (50%) of the basic wage. If an employee is required to work on their designated weekly rest day (Friday), the employer must provide an alternative rest day during the following week or pay the basic wage plus a premium of at least one hundred and fifty percent (150%).

Qatar Statutory Overtime Hourly Rates
Hourly Basic Wage = Monthly Basic Salary / (30 × 8)
Daytime Overtime Rate = Hourly Basic Wage × 1.25
Nighttime Overtime Rate = Hourly Basic Wage × 1.50
Weekly Rest Day Overtime Rate = Hourly Basic Wage × 2.50

4. Forfeiture Conditions Under Article 61

End-of-service gratuity is a strictly protected right under Qatar law. An employer may only dismiss a worker without notice and without gratuity under the narrow circumstances enumerated in Article 61. These include:

  • Assumption of false identity or submission of falsified documents;
  • Committing gross misconduct that inflicts severe financial loss on the employer, provided the employer notifies the Ministry of Labour within three days;
  • Repeated, documented violations of written health and safety instructions;
  • Breach of contractual confidentiality causing demonstrable commercial damage;
  • Intoxication or narcotics consumption during working hours.

In all standard terminations, restructuring, or voluntary employee departures after one year, full gratuity remains mandatory.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the statutory minimum gratuity rate in Qatar under Law No. 14/2004?
Under Article 54 of Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004, the statutory end-of-service gratuity is legally established at a minimum of three weeks (twenty-one calendar days) of basic wage for each completed continuous year of service after completing one full year.
Are partial years of service calculated pro-rata in Qatar?
Yes, under Article 54 of Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004, fractional parts of a service year are calculated on an exact pro-rata basis, ensuring workers receive compensation for all months and days served beyond completed calendar years.
What are the statutory overtime pay rates in Qatar?
Under Article 74 of Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004, regular overtime is compensated at basic wage plus not less than 25% (125%). For overtime worked between 9:00 PM and 3:00 AM or on weekly rest days (Friday), the statutory rate is basic wage plus 50% (150%).
Does an expatriate worker forfeit gratuity if they resign in Qatar?
No, under Article 54 of Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004, an expatriate employee who completes at least one year of continuous service is entitled to their statutory end-of-service gratuity upon resignation, provided statutory notice under Article 39 was served.
What is the maximum statutory limit for weekly working hours in Qatar?
Under Article 73 of Qatar Labour Law No. 14 of 2004, standard maximum working hours are forty-eight (48) hours per week (eight hours per day). During the holy month of Ramadan, statutory working hours are reduced to thirty-six (36) hours per week (six hours per day).
