How to Calculate GOSI Contributions in Saudi Arabia: Expat Rules, Annuity Branch Rates, and Wage Caps

Under Saudi GOSI regulations, social insurance contributions for Saudi nationals involve a combined 21.5% to 22% rate split between employer and employee covering annuity, work injuries, and unemployment, whereas expatriates are strictly subject to a 2% occupational hazard contribution paid entirely by the employer.
Published by GulfCalc Financial & Labour Law Editorial Team • November 8, 2026 • 8 min read • Statute: Saudi Royal Decree No. M/33 and Royal Decree No. M/211

The General Organization for Social Insurance (GOSI) administers the mandatory social security and workplace protections framework across the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Established under Royal Decree No. M/33 dated 3/9/1421H, the social insurance system governs compulsory monthly deductions for every operating commercial entity in the private sector. Whether managing a local corporate enterprise, establishing a foreign commercial branch under the Ministry of Investment, or auditing corporate payroll ledgers, compliance with GOSI statutory schedules is a foundational regulatory requirement.

Miscalculating social insurance obligations exposes commercial entities to strict financial penalties under Article 19 of the Social Insurance Law, including retroactive collection assessments and late compliance fines of two percent per month of unpaid dues. Core GOSI Contribution Rates: Saudi Nationals vs. Expatriates

The most fundamental structural distinction within the Saudi social insurance regime lies in nationality classification. GOSI enforces distinct statutory coverage schemes for Saudi national personnel compared to expatriate foreign workers. While Saudi nationals participate in comprehensive social protection branches covering retirement pensions, temporary unemployment security, and workplace accident protection, foreign workers are restricted to workplace injury indemnification.

For Saudi citizens employed in private establishments, social insurance represents a shared financial partnership between the employer and the employee. Deductions are taken directly from monthly payroll, while the commercial enterprise contributes an equal or greater statutory share. Conversely, for expatriate professionals, the employee pays zero percent out of pocket, and the sponsoring employer remits a flat statutory levy directly to GOSI on their behalf.

Breaking Down the Annuity, Unemployment (Saned), and Hazard Branches

The complete Saudi social insurance portfolio operates across three distinct legislative branches:

  • The Annuity Branch (Pensions / نظام المعاشات): Governed by Chapter 3 of the Social Insurance Law, this branch provides old-age pensions, disability support, and survivor benefits for heirs. The statutory contribution equals eighteen percent (18%) of the contributory wage, divided equally: nine percent (9%) deducted from the Saudi employee salary, and nine percent (9%) remitted by the sponsoring employer. Expatriate workers are strictly excluded from the Annuity Branch.
  • The Unemployment Insurance Branch (SANED / ساند): Promulgated under Royal Decree No. M/18, the Saned program provides interim financial allowances to Saudi employees who suffer involuntary job separation. The current statutory rate is one point five percent (1.5%) of the contributory salary, divided equally: zero point seven five percent (0.75%) withheld from the Saudi employee, and zero point seven five percent (0.75%) funded by the employer. Expatriates do not contribute to or benefit from Saned.
  • The Occupational Hazards Branch (أخطار مهنية): Codified under Chapter 4 of the statute, this branch provides comprehensive medical coverage, physical rehabilitation, and financial compensation for occupational illnesses, work-related accidents, and transit casualties. The contribution rate is fixed at two percent (2%) of the contributory wage. Crucially, this two percent is paid entirely by the employer for all personnel, both Saudi citizens and expatriates alike.

For an expatriate employee, the total insurance cost equals exactly 2.0%, paid entirely by the employing enterprise without any deduction from the foreign worker salary.

How to Determine Wage Caps and Contributory Salary Limits under GOSI

A frequent point of error in GCC payroll operations is assuming that social insurance contributions apply to an employee gross take-home pay. Under GOSI regulations, statutory percentages are applied exclusively to a strictly defined legal metric known as the Contributory Wage (الأجر الخاضع للاشتراك).

According to Ministerial Implementing Regulations issued pursuant to the Social Insurance Law, the contributory wage encompasses two components only:

  1. Basic Salary (الراتب الأساسي): The fixed contractual remuneration agreed upon in the employment contract registered on the Qiwa platform.
  2. Housing Allowance (بدل السكن): The cash stipend provided to the employee for housing accommodation. If the employer provides physical housing in kind rather than cash, GOSI administrative rules value the housing allowance at the equivalent of two months basic salary divided by twelve (or 16.67% of monthly basic pay).

All other supplementary allowances are legally excluded from the GOSI calculation base. This includes transportation allowances, mobile telephone stipends, children education grants, hardship subsidies, overtime compensation, and variable annual bonuses. In addition, sales commissions and performance percentages are subject to specific registration criteria and must be declared in annual GOSI updates.

To maintain fiscal stability and prevent excessive public liability, GOSI enforces statutory wage thresholds across all participating companies:

  • The Statutory Maximum Wage Cap: The ceiling on contributory wages is fixed at forty-five thousand Saudi Riyals (45,000 SAR) per month. Regardless of whether an executive receives a basic salary and housing allowance sum of 60,000 SAR or 120,000 SAR, GOSI contributions are calculated strictly on the maximum limit of 45,000 SAR. Any salary amount earned above this ceiling is exempt from social insurance levies.
  • The Statutory Minimum Contributory Floor: For private sector workers, the absolute minimum contributory floor recognized by GOSI is one thousand five hundred Saudi Riyals (1,500 SAR) per month. However, under Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) Nitaqat localization rules, a Saudi employee must receive a minimum monthly wage of 4,000 SAR to count as one full point toward the enterprise Saudization quota.

Employer and Employee Share Calculations: Step-by-Step Mathematical Breakdown

To understand how these principles translate into operational accounting, let us examine two practical calculation models reflecting realistic compensation structures in Riyadh and Jeddah.

Case Study 1: Calculating GOSI for a Saudi National Employee

Consider a Saudi finance manager working in a private Riyadh firm with the following contractual compensation package:

  • Basic Salary: 16,000 SAR
  • Housing Allowance: 4,000 SAR
  • Transportation Allowance: 1,500 SAR (Exempt)
  • Total Monthly Remuneration: 21,500 SAR

Step 1: Calculate Contributory Wage Base
Contributory Wage = Basic Salary + Housing Allowance = 16,000 SAR + 4,000 SAR = 20,000 SAR.
Note: The 1,500 SAR transportation allowance is completely excluded. The 20,000 SAR figure falls safely below the 45,000 SAR statutory ceiling.

Step 2: Calculate Employee Payroll Withholding
Employee Annuity Deduction (9%): 20,000 SAR × 0.09 = 1,800.00 SAR
Employee Saned Deduction (0.75%): 20,000 SAR × 0.0075 = 150.00 SAR
Total Employee Deduction: 1,800.00 SAR + 150.00 SAR = 1,950.00 SAR (9.75%)
Net Salary Received by Worker: 21,500 SAR - 1,950 SAR = 19,550.00 SAR.

Step 3: Calculate Employer Statutory Contribution
Employer Annuity Share (9%): 20,000 SAR × 0.09 = 1,800.00 SAR
Employer Saned Share (0.75%): 20,000 SAR × 0.0075 = 150.00 SAR
Occupational Hazards Share (2%): 20,000 SAR × 0.02 = 400.00 SAR
Total Employer Liability: 1,800.00 SAR + 150.00 SAR + 400.00 SAR = 2,350.00 SAR (11.75%).

Step 4: Remit Total Monthly Sum to GOSI
Total Remittance = Employee Share (1,950 SAR) + Employer Share (2,350 SAR) = 4,300.00 SAR (21.5% of contributory wage).

Case Study 2: Calculating GOSI for an Expatriate Employee

Now examine an expatriate software engineer employed at the same technology enterprise under the following terms:

  • Basic Salary: 14,000 SAR
  • Housing Allowance: 3,500 SAR
  • Transportation Allowance: 1,200 SAR (Exempt)
  • Total Monthly Remuneration: 18,700 SAR

Step 1: Calculate Contributory Wage Base
Contributory Wage = 14,000 SAR + 3,500 SAR = 17,500 SAR.

Step 2: Employee Payroll Withholding
Because foreign workers do not participate in Annuity or Saned schemes, employee withholding equals 0.00 SAR. The worker receives their full salary of 18,700 SAR without any insurance deductions.

Step 3: Employer Statutory Liability
The sponsoring enterprise remits strictly the Occupational Hazards fee:
Occupational Hazards (2%): 17,500 SAR × 0.02 = 350.00 SAR.
Total Employer Remittance to GOSI: 350.00 SAR per month.

While expatriates do not accrue GOSI pension rights, their terminal financial separation is governed by statutory gratuity under Articles 84 and 85 of the Saudi Labor Law. Recent Social Insurance Law Reforms and Private Sector Impacts

In mid-2024, the Kingdom enacted landmark statutory amendments through Royal Decree No. M/211 dated 25/12/1445H, modernizing the entire social insurance architecture. The updated legislation reflects Saudi Vision 2030 fiscal sustainability goals, aligning demographic longevity patterns with long-term retirement fund stability.

The 2024 reform establishes a distinct two-tier legislative regime:

  • Tier 1: Existing Contributors and Protected Cohorts: Saudi workers who entered the labor market prior to July 3, 2024 (27 Dhul-Hijjah 1445H) and possess existing contribution periods remain under the legacy Social Insurance Law framework. Those who have attained fifty Hijri years of age or accumulated twenty years of insured service retain their statutory early retirement milestones and previous benefit formulas without reduction.
  • Tier 2: New Labor Market Entrants: Citizens who entered registered employment on or after July 3, 2024, are subject to the modernized statutory scheme. Under the new rules, the standard retirement age transitions gradually from fifty-eight to sixty-five years depending on age cohort. Additionally, the minimum contribution period required to qualify for early pension entitlement increases incrementally from twenty-five to thirty years of verified service.

Additionally, the 2024 reform implements an incremental increase in the statutory Annuity Branch contribution rate for new entrants. Beginning in the second year following enactment, the Annuity contribution rate rises by zero point five percent (0.5%) annually across four consecutive years, moving from eighteen percent up to twenty-two percent (split equally between employer and employee at 11% each). Sponsoring enterprises must account for these scheduled rate escalations in multi-year corporate budget projections.

While Saudi Arabia operates through GOSI, neighboring jurisdictions enforce distinct tax and social protection frameworks. Frequently Asked Questions

What components of an employee compensation package are included in the GOSI contributory wage?

The GOSI contributory wage consists strictly of the employee basic salary plus contractual housing allowance. If housing is provided in kind, it is valued at two months basic salary divided by twelve. Commission payments and sales percentages can be included under specific circumstances. Variable allowances like transportation, telephone stipends, overtime pay, and annual performance bonuses are strictly excluded from the contributory wage base.

Are expatriate employees entitled to receive an annuity pension from GOSI upon retirement?

No. Expatriate personnel do not contribute to or participate in the GOSI Annuity Branch or the Saned unemployment program. Non-Saudi workers are covered exclusively under the Occupational Hazards Branch, which is financed entirely by an employer-paid two percent levy. Expatriates receive statutory End of Service Benefits from their employers under Articles 84 and 85 of the Saudi Labor Law upon departure.

What is the maximum statutory salary cap for GOSI contributions in Saudi Arabia?

The statutory maximum contributory wage under GOSI is forty-five thousand Saudi Riyals (45,000 SAR) per month. Any monthly basic salary and housing allowance sum exceeding this threshold is exempt from further social insurance deductions across Annuity, Saned, and Occupational Hazard branches.

How does the Saned unemployment insurance scheme divide contributions between employers and employees?

Under current Royal Decrees governing the Saned system, the total monthly contribution rate is one point five percent (1.5%) of the contributory wage. This is divided equally as zero point seven five percent (0.75%) deducted from the Saudi employee salary and zero point seven five percent (0.75%) paid by the employer. Saned applies exclusively to Saudi national personnel under sixty years of age.

What are the key changes introduced by the 2024 Social Insurance Law reform regarding contribution rates and retirement age?

Promulgated under Royal Decree No. M/211, the revised Social Insurance Law applies to new labor market entrants who joined on or after July 3, 2024. For these new contributors, the statutory retirement age gradually rises from fifty-eight to sixty-five years, and Annuity contributions increase by zero point five percent annually over four years from eighteen percent up to twenty-two percent. Existing contributors with sufficient service retain legacy retirement benchmarks.
