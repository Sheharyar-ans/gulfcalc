Saudi Labor Law Amendments: The New 30-Day Notice Period, Actual Wage Gratuity, and 180-Day Probation Rules

The Saudi labor law amendments establish that employee resignation notice is 30 days while employer termination notice requires 60 days for indefinite contracts. Contractual probation extends up to 180 days, overtime can be converted into compensatory leave, and end-of-service gratuity remains calculated on actual comprehensive wage.
Published by GulfCalc Legal Analysis Team • October 27, 2026 • 7 min read • Statute: Saudi Royal Decree No. M/51 Amendments

The modernization of Saudi Arabian employment jurisprudence under Vision 2030 has produced substantial revisions to Royal Decree No. M/51. Promulgated through Cabinet decisions and Royal Decree amendments, these statutory updates reshape private sector workforce management across the Kingdom. Supervised by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD), the revised provisions establish calibrated rules for termination notices, contractual probation terms, overtime remuneration, and terminal severance entitlements.

For enterprise counsel and payroll leaders, compliance requires a rigorous understanding of how these revised articles operate in practice. Practitioners seeking comparative regional analysis can reference our central GCC Labour Laws Directory.

Revised Notice Periods Under Article 75: 30 Days for Workers vs. 60 Days for Employers

Originally, Article 75 of Royal Decree No. M/51 mandated symmetrical notice periods for indefinite employment agreements. Either party terminating an indefinite contract for valid cause was bound by an identical sixty-day notice window for monthly salaried personnel. The legislative revisions replace this symmetry with an asymmetrical notice model.

For indefinite contracts with monthly salary schedules, the revised Article 75 establishes:

  • Employee Resignation Notice (30 Days): When a worker initiates termination, the statutory notice period is thirty (30) days. This lower threshold facilitates labor mobility, reduces career transition friction, and accelerates recruitment cycles.
  • Employer Termination Notice (60 Days): When an employer terminates an indefinite contract for a valid cause, a minimum notice period of sixty (60) days remains mandatory. This affords workers reasonable runway to secure alternative employment or transfer sponsorship via Qiwa.
  • Non-Monthly Wage Earners (15 Days): For employees compensated on daily, weekly, or piece-work schedules, the notice duration is fifteen (15) days for both parties.

Under Article 76, non-observance of statutory notice requires the defaulting party to pay compensation equal to the employee's wage for the unserved notice duration, calculated on the full actual wage baseline.

Resignation Handling and the 60-Day Employer Delay Mechanism

To prevent operational disruption resulting from the shorter 30-day resignation notice, the amended regulatory framework establishes a structured postponement procedure. When an employee submits a resignation, it takes effect upon the conclusion of the 30-day notice period by default.

However, if business necessity requires an extended handover, the employer holds a statutory right to postpone the effective date of the resignation by up to sixty (60) days from the initial submission date. The employer must communicate the operational justification in writing. Throughout this postponement window, the employment relationship continues under full compensation, ensuring complete salary disbursal, continuous social insurance coverage, and active health benefits.

Actual Wage vs. Basic Wage in End-of-Service Calculations (Articles 84 and 85)

The End of Service Award (Mukafoat Nihayat Al-Khidma) remains anchored in the statutory concept of "Actual Wage" (Al-Ajr Al-Fi'li). Computing terminal gratuity exclusively on basic salary (Al-Ajr Al-Asasi) is consistently invalidated in Saudi labor court proceedings.

Article 2 of the Saudi Labor Law defines compensation through two distinct baselines:

  • Basic Wage: Core contractual remuneration agreed upon between the parties, excluding supplementary benefits and allowances.
  • Actual Wage: The basic wage plus all regular, recurring cash additions provided in consideration of labor, specifically including contractual housing and transportation allowances.

Article 84 mandates computing end-of-service awards on latest drawn actual wage, voiding any contractual waiver under Article 8. Payroll teams can verify liability using our Saudi End of Service Calculator, and evaluate insurance caps via the GOSI contribution calculator.

The 180-Day Probation Period Framework (Article 53)

Under previous rules, Article 53 capped initial probation at ninety days, requiring a mid-term agreement to reach 180 days. The amended statute streamlines this procedure:

  • Initial Contractual Agreement Upfront: Parties may agree directly in the initial employment contract to a probation term of up to 180 days, removing mid-probation administrative burdens.
  • Statutory Ceiling: Total probation duration cannot exceed 180 calendar days with the same employer. Longer contractual terms are void for the excess duration.
  • Statutory Exclusions: Official Eid holidays and verified sick leaves under Article 117 pause the probationary timeline, which resumes upon return to active work.
  • Separation Without Severance: During probation, either party may terminate without notice or indemnity under Article 77, creating no end-of-service award liability.
  • Repeated Probation Restrictions: Repeated probation with the same employer is barred unless six months have elapsed, or the employee is hired in a different capacity.

Overtime Compensation: Cash Uplift vs. Compensatory Time-Off in Lieu

Standard working hours are capped under Article 98 at eight hours daily or forty-eight hours weekly, reduced during Ramadan for Muslim workers. Hours worked beyond statutory limits constitute overtime under Article 107.

The amendments modernize Article 107 through two distinct settlement channels:

  • Statutory Cash Uplift: Default overtime requires payment equal to the normal hourly rate plus a 50% uplift on basic salary.
  • Compensatory Time-Off Option: The law authorizes paid compensatory leave in lieu of overtime pay.
  • Written Consent Prerequisite: Compensatory time-off requires documented written consent from the worker and cannot be unilaterally mandated.
  • Separation Reconciliation: Unused compensatory leave must be liquidated into cash at statutory overtime rates upon final settlement.

Consider an expatriate engineer tendering voluntary resignation in Riyadh under the amended framework.

Separation Mode: Voluntary resignation submitted via Qiwa
Basic Salary: 12,000 SAR per month

Transport Allowance: 1,000 SAR per month
Actual Monthly Wage: 16,000 SAR
Terminal Determination
Statutory Payout Calculation
Notice Served (Article 75): 30 days mandatory notice served
Notice Wage Remitted: 16,000 SAR (regular salary during notice)
Gross Accrual (Article 84): 4 × (16,000 / 2) = 32,000 SAR
Resignation Ratio (Article 85): 2 to 5 years bracket = 33.33% (one-third)
Statutory Forfeiture: 66.67% deducted due to voluntary resignation
Net Payable Gratuity: 10,666.67 SAR

The settlement proceeds through three defined legal steps:

  1. Actual Wage Establishment: Pursuant to Article 2, payroll aggregates basic pay (12,000 SAR), housing (3,000 SAR), and transportation (1,000 SAR) to establish an actual monthly wage of 16,000 SAR. Computing on basic wage alone would unlawfully underpay the engineer by 2,666.67 SAR.
  2. Tenure Accrual Calculation: Under Article 84, service during the first five years accrues at half of one month's actual wage annually: 4 × 8,000 SAR = 32,000 SAR gross accrual.
  3. Resignation Tier Application: Under Article 85, voluntary resignation between two and five years entitles the worker to one-third (33.33%) vesting: 32,000 × (1/3) = 10,666.67 SAR. Under Article 88, the employer must settle all final dues within two weeks of departure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the statutory notice period for employee resignation under the amended Saudi Labor Law?
Under amended Article 75 of Royal Decree No. M/51, an employee resigning from an indefinite monthly contract must provide 30 days notice. In contrast, employer termination requires 60 days notice.
Does the 180-day probation period apply automatically to all new employment contracts?
No. Default probation remains 90 days under Article 53. Extending probation up to the 180-day ceiling requires explicit written agreement between the parties in the contract or via written amendment.
Why must end-of-service gratuity be calculated on actual wage rather than basic salary?
Article 84 requires gratuity calculations based on actual wage, which under Article 2 includes regular housing and transport allowances. Under Article 8, any clause excluding allowances from gratuity is legally void.
How does the compensatory time-off in lieu option function for overtime work?
Under amended Article 107, overtime can be converted into paid compensatory leave instead of cash pay, provided the employee grants voluntary written consent.
Can an employer postpone the effective date of an employee's resignation under the new rules?
Yes. An employer may postpone the resignation date by up to 60 days if operational necessity exists, with written notice to the worker and full remuneration maintained.
