Institutional Statement: Accuracy, Legal Grounding & Mission

1. Our Institutional Mission

Founded with the objective of eliminating opacity in regional labour and commercial calculations, GulfCalc (gulfcalc.tech) serves as the premier, independent mathematical computing platform for expatriate professionals, human resource directors, accountants, and legal practitioners operating across the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC).

The GCC represents one of the most dynamic cross-border labor markets on earth, with non-national workers constituting upwards of 70% to 90% of the private-sector workforce in several member states. Despite the economic scale of this ecosystem, existing digital calculators have historically been plagued by algorithmic errors, outdated statutory baselines, invasive tracking scripts, and non-compliant arithmetic formulas.

2. Computational Rigor & Legal Source Verification

Every computational kernel deployed on GulfCalc is audited against official ministerial gazettes, royal decrees, and primary legislative codes:

  • State of Kuwait: Private Sector Labour Law No. 6 of 2010, strictly incorporating the mandatory Article 55 daily divisor of 26 working days (excluding statutory weekly rest days) and the 18-month statutory cap under Article 51.
  • Kingdom of Saudi Arabia: Royal Decree No. M/51 (Saudi Labour Law), validating that calculations benchmark against the comprehensive Actual Wage (Basic + Housing + Transport) under Articles 84, 85, and 87.
  • United Arab Emirates: Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 on the Regulation of Labour Relations and Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 on Corporate Taxation.
  • State of Qatar: Law No. 14 of 2004 (Article 54) and Ministry of Labour directives guaranteeing a minimum 21 days basic salary per year.
  • Sultanate of Oman: Royal Decree No. 53/2023 (effective July 2023), reflecting the modern flat 30-day accrual standard for non-Omani workers.
  • Kingdom of Bahrain: Law No. 36 of 2012 (Article 116) and 2024 Social Insurance Organisation (SIO) expat indemnity deposit requirements.

3. Zero-Telemetry Client-Side Architecture

In an era of ubiquitous corporate tracking and data profiling, GulfCalc adheres to an uncompromising privacy philosophy: Zero Data Transmission.

When an employee or employer enters salary data, length of service, or tax margins into our calculators:

  • No input values are ever sent over HTTP or WebSockets to our servers.
  • No database or remote session log records your financial figures.
  • All arithmetic transformations are executed locally in real-time inside your browser's JavaScript V8/JavaScriptCore virtual machine.
  • You can inspect our open-source codebase directly on our verified GitHub repository: github.com/Sheharyar-ans/gulfcalc.

4. Review Cycle & Legislative Tracking

Labor laws and tax thresholds in the GCC are actively evolving as part of broader national transformation programs (e.g., Saudi Vision 2030, UAE Centennial 2071, Oman Vision 2040, and Kuwait Vision 2035). Our legal engineering team continuously monitors the official gazettes (Al-Kuwait Al-Yawm, Umm Al-Qura, UAE Federal Gazette, Oman Official Gazette) to push instant patches whenever ministerial executive regulations are amended.
