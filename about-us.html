About GulfCalc: Methodology, Legal Grounding & Mission

1. Founder & Project Mission

Created by software engineer Sheharyar Siraj (sheharyarsiraj.com), GulfCalc (gulfcalc.tech) is an independent, non-governmental computational platform built to provide transparent, mathematically accurate calculations for expatriates, HR professionals, and businesses operating across the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC).

The GCC represents one of the most dynamic cross-border labor markets globally, with non-national workers constituting a substantial proportion of the private-sector workforce across the six member nations. Despite the scale of this workforce, existing online tools have often suffered from outdated statutory assumptions, lack of statutory citations, or intrusive tracking. GulfCalc was designed as an open, accessible, client-side resource.

2. Computational Rigor & Primary Statutory Sources

Each calculator on GulfCalc benchmarks against primary legislative texts, ministerial decisions, and official executive decrees:

  • State of Kuwait: Private Sector Labour Law No. 6 of 2010, applying the mandatory Article 55 daily divisor of 26 working days and the 18-month statutory cap under Article 51.
  • Kingdom of Saudi Arabia: Royal Decree No. M/51 (Saudi Labour Law), validating that calculations benchmark against the comprehensive Actual Wage (Basic + Housing + Transport) under Articles 84, 85, and 87.
  • United Arab Emirates: Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 on the Regulation of Labour Relations and Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 on Corporate Taxation.
  • State of Qatar: Law No. 14 of 2004 (Article 54) establishing a statutory minimum of 21 days basic salary per continuous year.
  • Sultanate of Oman: Royal Decree No. 53/2023 (New Labour Law), reflecting the flat 30-day accrual standard for non-Omani workers.
  • Kingdom of Bahrain: Law No. 36 of 2012 (Article 116) and Ministerial Decision No. 109 of 2023 regarding Social Insurance Organisation (SIO) expat indemnity deposits.

3. Zero-Telemetry Client-Side Architecture

GulfCalc follows a strict client-side computation model for user financial inputs:

  • No salary, tenure, or corporate financial inputs are transmitted over HTTP/WebSockets to external servers.
  • No remote databases or user session logs record your calculation numbers.
  • All arithmetic transformations are executed locally in real-time inside your web browser.
  • The codebase is publicly inspectable on GitHub: github.com/Sheharyar-ans/gulfcalc.

4. Legislative Monitoring & Continuous Verification

Labor laws and tax regulations across the GCC evolve continuously as part of national transformation agendas (including Saudi Vision 2030, UAE Centennial 2071, and Oman Vision 2040). We regularly review official government gazettes (such as Al-Kuwait Al-Yawm, Umm Al-Qura, UAE Federal Gazette, and Oman Official Gazette) to keep computational algorithms aligned whenever executive regulations or statutory articles are amended.
